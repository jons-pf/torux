torux 0.3.0

- An island chain's width can be measured instead of estimated. With `largest=True`, `widths_s=S` (off by default, its own seconds after the search, also `torux.many(..., widths_s=S)` and `--widths S` on the command line) closes in on every chain of the answer that has an O or an X line, with direct requests at fluxes between the verified surfaces either side. Each such chain then carries `bracket` in `answer.evidence['chains']`: `inner_Wb` and `outer_Wb`, the verified surfaces nearest it either side, which hold its islands and its chaotic layer between them. A surface's side is decided by the chain's closed lines' crossings against the surface's section, never by iota, so a profile that turns back does not mislead it. A chain with no verified surface beyond it is reported one-sided (`one_sided`, `outer_Wb` None). `stop` says per side why the closing in stopped: `closed` (under 1 % of the bracket is left between the verified surface and the nearest request that did not verify), `time`, `requests`, or that no verified surface lies beyond the chain. `closed` says how near torux could bring a surface, not how near the island is. `widths_jobs=N` runs N requests at once, each on cores of its own on Linux. On macOS they share the cores, and everywhere each takes its own memory. Where a request of the closing in verifies a surface beyond the search's answer, the largest such surface becomes the answer and its words say so. `torux.many`'s table gains a `chain_brackets` column when widths are asked, and `--widths-jobs` without `--widths` is an error.
- The search for the largest surface can now get past an island chain its families cannot cross. Where the family stalls at a chain and nothing certified bounds the coils, it traces field lines beyond the chain, builds a torus from a regular one, and asks for surfaces from there. Every surface it returns is refined, guarded and independently verified as any other. On the 79 CNT configurations it found a torus beyond a chain in 9. It does not run once a bound is certified, so the same coils can pass a chain in one run and stop at it in another. What it did is in `answer.evidence['search']['traced']` and in the answer's words.
- Every closed field line an answer stores says how well it closes: `closure_m` (how far it comes back after its q periods), `distance_m` (Newton's step from the first stored crossing, or what the tracer's error leaves undetermined where that is larger: an upper bound on the distance to the closed line, loose by many orders on a thin chain) and `converged` (`distance_m` and `closure_m` both at most 1e-6 m). This covers chain lines, the record and every certificate's lines. Lines the edge barrier screened on its coarse map are followed on with Newton to their closed line (`polished` says how far they moved). The line an edge barrier's certificate rests on is followed on the same way, within the certificate's own bound on its distance from the true line, `polished` keeping it as the certificate used it. A certificate's other lines are measured and not moved. A line is listed once per chain. In 0.2.2, 21 of 157 lines found only by an edge barrier on CNT had a residue more than 1 % off. A line that neither converges nor closes to 1e-7 m is left out and counted in `lines_not_converged`.
- A chain line's residue is its line's, with an estimate of its error. Each line solved as a closed curve is solved again with more Fourier modes until two resolutions agree, and `residue_err` is what they still differ by, never below 1e-12: an estimate, which extended-precision tracing has seen exceeded by up to 1.6e-11. Lines found only by an edge barrier, and the lines its certificate rests on, carry no `residue_err`: their residues are right to about 1e-4. On 56 QUASR designs, half of them of six field periods, 0.2.2 stored X and O residues up to 18 % off while recording a tiny error. A residue within its error gives no type and no half width: such lines are listed under `unresolved`, not as O or X lines. The chains a family steps across have their lines found as in 0.2.2 and then refined in an allowance of the same length after it (`lines_unrefined` counts a line the time did not reach).
- A chain the family stalls at whose O line Newton does not find (it ended on the magnetic axis) is searched for its stellarator-symmetric O line by tracing, which on CNT finds 13 of 34. O lines that form a mirror pair off the symmetry lines are not sought. `lines_traced` counts the lines found so.
- At a stall the chain named is the rational of order at most 30 the family meets first beyond its last torus (`stalled_at` true, `stalled_from` the request and the iota and flux of that family's last torus). 0.2.2 named the one of lowest order ahead, which could lie beyond another. The jump and the certificates still go on from the lowest-order one, listed with `stalled_at` false. Family rows give `family_iota`, the transform their flux is extrapolated from.
- The edge barrier keeps a closed line beyond the last torus when every crossing lies outside the torus's section. 0.2.2 asked for a ray ratio above 1, which drops lines in a fold of a section that is not star-shaped about the axis.
- `torux.poincare` counts a line round a magnetic axis as gone only once it is half the conductors' extent beyond the box round them, so islands and surfaces that reach past the conductors are drawn.
- No chain band in an answer of the largest search holds a surface the same answer verified. Every band (`flux_Wb` plus or minus `half_width_Wb`, and the bracket where widths were asked) is held against every surface the answer verified, placed by the chain's crossings. A band that held one is flagged (`band_held_verified_Wb`) and cut on that side to the surface (`band_cut_Wb`). After a cut `flux_Wb` and `half_width_Wb` are the middle and half of what is left, no longer the interpolated flux and the pendulum width, and `flux_from` says where the estimate (`flux_estimate_Wb`, `half_width_pendulum_Wb`) came from. Where the side cannot be told, no band is given and `band_withdrawn` says why. With widths asked about half the estimates are withdrawn and the bracket stands in their place. The summary is in `answer.evidence['search']['bands']`. A verified torus that a request's family reached and no direct request verified again is named as such in the words.
- The Boozer printout names its quasi-helical number for what it is: SIMSOPT's NonQuasiSymmetricRatio of the surface parametrised in QUASR's helical frame. No number changes.

Known limits

- A chain's flux, where widths were not asked, is still interpolated in iota between family tori. Direct requests at fluxes inside such a band often verify a surface the chain's crossings place clear of it: the band gives the chain's width, not its place. The bracket bounds its place.
- Brackets close tight only with time and cores. On two cores and an hour, most sides stop on time: the bracket is then sound but loose. Brackets of thin chains, whose lines torux cannot call converged, can be withdrawn where a verified surface passes too near every crossing to place.
- The chains a family steps across now take an allowance twice as long as in 0.2.2 after a search that used its whole budget: up to 90 s to find their lines and up to 90 s more to refine their residues. On CNT at 1200 s an answer ran up to 142 s past its budget, where 0.2.2 ran up to 67 s.
- Tracing beyond a chain takes its time from the search's budget and changes the fluxes asked after it. In the same budget the largest surface can so come out smaller than 0.2.2's: CNT at 64 degrees, ratio 6.0 gave 5.42e-3 Wb in 3 of 6 runs on slower machines and 5.99e-3 in the other 3, where 0.2.2 gave 5.79e-3 in 6 of 6, and at 88 degrees, ratio 5.5 its requests take about 3 % longer and it reached 0.2.2's last step (1.341e-4 against 1.263e-4 Wb) in 9 of 12 runs where 0.2.2 did in 6 of 6. At 2400 s both gave the larger value.
- A surface the largest search reaches beyond a chain from traced lines may not be reached by a direct request at the same flux, whose family still stops at the chain.
- The answer's surface is the largest a direct request verified. A verified torus that the family of a request reached can lie beyond it (on CNT the no-flux request's, in 4 of 79 answers), and the chains may be placed against it. The words name it as such.
- Memory: peak memory can be up to about twice 0.2.2's, on thin chains whose lines are refined to many Fourier modes (Hammond's second wireframe: 2.7 to 3.5 GB against 1.5 to 1.9 GB, CNT 78 degrees, ratio 5.2: 4.5 GB against 3.6 to 4.0 GB). Each of `widths_jobs` requests adds its own.
- An edge barrier's certificate depends on the machine's speed: on CNT the same request is certified on faster processors and not on slower ones, in 0.2.2 alike. A bound missing from one run is not evidence against it.
- A closed line in a fold of the last torus's section is listed but not tried as a barrier, so no certificate rests on it.
- The default `torux.poincare` call may still put no line in an island chain that lies beyond lines that leave. Starts given with `starts=` show it.
- Which tori a family lands on depends on the machine's timing, so another run can give another largest surface, other verified surfaces either side of a chain and other brackets. Where the independent check of a surface sits near its threshold, the verdict can depend on the processor.
